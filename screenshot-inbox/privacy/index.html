Screenshot Inbox

Privacy Policy

Effective 6 September 2026 · Last updated 6 September 2026

Screenshot Inbox turns your screenshots into reminders, events, saved links, contacts and searchable records, and then helps you delete the originals. This policy explains what it reads, what stays on your device, and the very short list of things that ever leave it. It describes the app as it actually behaves.

The short version. No account. No server of ours, anywhere. Your screenshots are read on your device by Apple's own on-device text recognition, and neither the images nor the text taken out of them are sent to us — we have nowhere to send them to. There is no advertising and no analytics service of any kind. Two things can leave your device, both optional and both described below: an address you saved, when the app fetches that page's title, and your saved items, if you turn on iCloud Sync. Nothing is ever deleted from your photo library without you confirming it first.

2. What the app reads

With your permission, the app reads screenshots from your photo library. It looks only at images iOS has marked as screenshots, and ignores your photographs. You can also hand it specific images yourself, through the picker or the share sheet, in which case it reads what you gave it and nothing else.

Reading happens entirely on your device, using Apple's Vision and Natural Language frameworks, which are part of iOS. A screenshot is never uploaded for processing. There is no cloud processing mode in this version; the setting that would gate one exists so that nothing can ever change that without you turning it on first, and it does nothing today.

If you grant limited photo access rather than full access, the app works with the images you selected and cannot see the rest of your library.

3. What stays on your device

All of the following are stored locally and are not transmitted to us:

  • Your screenshots, and copies of images you shared or imported
  • Text recognised in them, and its position on the image
  • Dates, addresses, prices, order and tracking numbers, links, phone numbers, email addresses and other details extracted from that text
  • The category the app decided a screenshot belongs to, and its confidence
  • Saved items, their notes, and the thumbnail and text kept with them
  • Collections, tags and automation rules you create
  • Return deadlines, warranty dates and other scheduled activity
  • Image fingerprints used to spot duplicates
  • Every setting, including which permissions you granted

The app keeps a copy of a screenshot's text and a small thumbnail alongside each saved item, deliberately. It is what lets the information survive after you delete the original, which is the entire point of the app.

Where a screenshot contains information about someone else — a business card, a message, an invitation — you are responsible for having the right to keep it. It is stored on your device the same as your own.

4. Deleting your screenshots

This is the app's most consequential action, so it is worth being exact.

  • Nothing is ever deleted without you confirming it, on a screen that says how many screenshots are involved and how many of them hold information you have not saved anywhere else. This cannot be turned off.
  • Deletion goes through iOS, which shows you its own confirmation as well. Both prompts are intentional.
  • Deleted screenshots go to Recently Deleted in Photos, where iOS keeps them for around 30 days. Until then you can restore them yourself.
  • What the app extracted is saved before the original is touched, and survives its deletion.
  • "Delete all data" in Settings erases the app's own database and images. It does not touch your photo library. Deleting your app data and deleting your photos are different requests, and the app treats them that way.

5. iCloud Sync

Off by default. With it off, nothing about your items leaves the device.

Turning it on copies your saved items — their titles, notes, extracted text, thumbnails, tags, collections and scheduled dates — into your own private iCloud database, under your Apple Account, so they survive a lost or reset device and appear on your other devices. Your original screenshots are not uploaded. We have no access to that data: it is held by Apple under Apple's privacy policy, in your account, not ours. We receive nothing and can retrieve nothing.

Turning sync off stops further copying. Removing what has already been copied is done through your Apple Account's iCloud storage settings, or by using "Delete all data" in the app while sync is on.

6. What else leaves your device

One thing, and only if you leave it on. When you save a link, the app asks that page for its title and icon so the saved item is recognisable. That request goes to the website itself, reveals your device's IP address to it as any web request does, and is the only outbound request the app makes on its own. It can be turned off completely in Settings under "Fetch link previews", and with it off the app makes no network request at all.

We operate no server for this app. There is no endpoint of ours for the app to talk to.

7. The apps you send things to

When you confirm a suggestion, the app writes to Calendar, Reminders, Contacts, Maps or your browser through iOS. It writes only the item you approved on screen, and only after you approve it. Once written, that information lives in that app and under its own rules and sync settings — a calendar event you approved will sync wherever your calendar syncs.

The app checks for an existing match before creating a contact or an event, so it can offer to update rather than create a duplicate. That check reads your Contacts and Calendar; it does not copy them.

8. Location

Only used for reminders you set up yourself. If you ask to be reminded about a screenshot when you arrive somewhere, the app asks iOS to watch for that one place, and iOS tells it when you arrive. Your location is never recorded, never stored, and never sent anywhere. The app does not track your movement, does not start continuous location updates, and keeps background location updates disabled. Remove the reminder and the app stops watching for that place.

9. Device search

Saved items are added to your iPhone's own search index so you can find them from the Home Screen. That index is created and held by iOS on your device and is not sent to us. Anything the app has flagged as sensitive is left out of it whatever your setting says, because search results can appear on a Lock Screen. You can turn indexing off entirely in Settings, which withdraws everything the app has published to it.

10. Sensitive screenshots

The app tries to notice when a screenshot holds something like a password, a one-time code or banking details, and marks it. Marked items can have their thumbnails hidden, are excluded from device search, and never appear in the body of a notification. This detection runs on your device and is a convenience, not a guarantee — it will miss things, and you should not rely on it to keep a secret.

11. What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no sale or sharing of personal data with advertisers.
  • No analytics or product-telemetry service of any kind. The app contains no analytics provider. What it calls analytics is written to your device's own system log and goes nowhere.
  • No third-party trackers, SDKs or advertising identifiers.
  • No account, no sign-in, no profile.
  • No uploading of your screenshots or the text in them, under any setting.
  • No use of your screenshots to train any model, ours or anyone else's.
  • No deleting anything from your photo library without an explicit confirmation.

12. Children

Screenshot Inbox is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from a child; in practice we do not collect personal data from anyone, because there is nowhere for it to go. If you believe a child has given us personal data directly, write to support@apsoluts.com and we will delete it.

13. Security and retention

The app's database and the images it stores are held in your device's app storage and protected by iOS file protection, which keeps them encrypted while the device is locked. You can additionally require Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode to open the app, and have it hide its contents in the app switcher.

We retain nothing, because we receive nothing. Your data lives for as long as you keep it on your device, plus whatever you have chosen to copy to your own iCloud account.

No system is perfectly secure and we do not claim otherwise. Having no account and no server limits what any breach of ours could expose — there is no store of your information to breach — but it does not protect your device itself if it is lost, stolen, or unlocked in someone else's hand.

14. Your rights

Everything the app holds is on your device and under your control. You can view, edit and delete any item in the app, export all of your saved items and their extracted text from Settings as a JSON file, and erase everything with "Delete all data".

Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to object to or restrict processing, to data portability, and to complain to a supervisory authority. If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, we do not process your personal data on our own systems at all; where the app processes it on your device, it does so to perform the features you asked for. We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising as those terms are used in California law.

15. International transfers

We operate from Texas, in the United States. Because we receive no personal data from the app, there is no transfer of your data to us to describe. If you turn on iCloud Sync, Apple processes that data under its own policy and its own arrangements.
